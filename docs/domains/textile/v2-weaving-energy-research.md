# TaqaMind V2 — Phase 1: Weaving Industrial Energy Research Report

**Scope:** Electrical energy consumption in industrial Weaving (with priority on Egyptian industrial conditions and traceable international benchmarks).

---

## 1. Industrial Workflow & Major Energy-Consuming Operations

1. **Warp Preparation (Winding, Warping, Sizing):**
   - Winding and warping consume mechanical motor power; **sizing** is thermally intensive due to steam/hot-air drying of sized warp yarns (LBNL-3970E).
2. **Loom Shedding, Beating-Up & Weft Insertion (Primary Electrical Load):**
   - Shedding, beat-up, warp let-off, and cloth take-up are driven by main/auxiliary electric motors.
   - **Weft insertion technology** dominates the electrical intensity differences across loom types.
3. **Climate Control & Auxiliary Utilities:**
   - Strict temperature and relative humidity (RH) control via air-washing/HVAC/humidification plants to maintain yarn strength and reduce warp breaks, plus high-bay lighting and pneumatic cleaning.

---

## 2. Loom Technologies & Reference Energy Benchmarks (International)

| Loom Technology | Weft Insertion Mechanism | Typical Machine Running Power (kW) | Specific Energy Consumption (kWh/kg fabric) | Primary Energy Driver |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Air-Jet Loom** | Main + relay compressed air nozzles | **3.5 – 6.0+ kW** (machine + compressor equivalent) | **2.0 – 5.0+ kWh/kg** (NPTEL/textile DB benchmark ~**3.2+ kWh/kg**) | **Compressed air (~80%** of weft insertion energy goes to main/relay nozzles) |
| **Rapier Loom** | Mechanical flexible/rigid rapier bands | **2.0 – 4.5 kW** | **1.5 – 3.5 kWh/kg** | Purely electrical/mechanical drive inertia (competitive on heavy / multi-color weaves) |
| **Water-Jet Loom** | High-pressure water stream (synthetics only) | **1.5 – 3.5 kW** | **~0.9 kWh/kg** | Water pump + main motor (most energy-efficient loom class) |

- **Broader Weaving Department Benchmark:** Including preparatory sub-processes (winding, warping, sizing), compressed air, lighting, and HVAC, composite weaving energy intensity reaches **5.5 – 6.0+ kWh/kg** or **0.6 – 1.8 kWh/meter** depending on fabric GSM and pick density.
- **Composite Mill Share (LBNL-3970E):** Weaving + preparation typically represents **~18% to 26%** of total electricity in integrated textile mills.

---

## 3. Key Operating Parameters Driving Weaving Electrical Energy

1. **Loom Speed (RPM / Picks per Minute):** Linearly scales compressed air volume on air-jet looms and mechanical losses on rapier looms.
2. **Fabric Width (Reed Space):** Wider looms require more active relay nozzles and higher air pressure (air-jet) or higher rapier mass/inertia (rapier).
3. **Warp & Weft Density (EPI / PPI) & Yarn Count / Material:** Higher pick density requires more picks (and thus more kWh) per linear meter; yarn hairiness and tensile strength affect stop rates and pneumatic drag.
4. **Compressed Air Demand & Compressor Specific Power:**
   - Compressor specific power typically ranges **0.15 – 0.25 kWh/Nm³**.
   - Relay/sub-nozzles account for **~80%** of air-jet consumption.
   - Every **1 bar** reduction in discharge pressure yields roughly **6–8%** compressor energy savings (LBNL-3970E).
5. **Machine Efficiency & Utilization:** Low efficiency (frequent warp/weft stops) leaves HVAC, humidification, lighting, and idling compressors running while output drops, spiking **kWh/kg**.

---

## 4. Egyptian Industrial Conditions (UNIDO / GEF / World Bank Evidence)

- **Motor Systems Dominance:** UNIDO & Global Efficiency Intelligence assessments of Egypt's manufacturing sector report that industrial motor-driven systems account for **>70% of manufacturing electricity consumption**, with pumps, fans, and compressed air representing **~45%** of motor electricity.
- **Efficiency Gap:** UNIDO/World Bank assessments indicate Egyptian industrial motor systems frequently consume **~20% more energy than international best practices** due to aging/oversized motors, low load factors, and unmanaged air leaks.
- **Compressed Air Savings Potential in Egypt:** UNIDO/GEI report **~39% technical electricity-savings potential** in Egyptian compressed air systems (translating to **>1,250 GWh/year** nationally). Under UNIDO MED-TEST II / CASO / MSO programs in Egypt, fixing leaks and optimizing compressor pressure/sequencing delivered payback periods **under 1 year**.
- **Regulatory & Economic Context:** Egypt's Ministerial Decree **#463/2020** introduced Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS) for electric motors, while industrial electricity tariff subsidy phase-outs make **kWh/kg** optimization critical for Egyptian mills.
